# The Lost Gold of El Dorado: A Comprehensive Adventure Game Guide

## Introduction and System Setup

Welcome, Game Master, to *The Lost Gold of El Dorado*, an epic, pulp-style adventure campaign designed for a party of 4 to 6 players. This comprehensive book acts as an interactive, non-linear blueprint for your campaign, providing deep setting details, action set-pieces, survival subsystems, travel hazards, and puzzle templates.

The premise of this adventure is deeply rooted in classic, high-octane exploration. The characters are a team of renowned archaeologists, rugged survivalists, and daring pilots in the year 1937. They have acquired the *Chronicles of Orellana*—an ancient Spanish journal that contains clues to the location of the legendary golden city of *Manóa*, more commonly known as El Dorado, hidden deep within the unexplored corners of the Amazon Basin. However, they are not alone in their quest. A ruthless syndicate of mercenaries and relic hunters known as the *Vanguard Group*, led by the fanatical commander *Klaus von Adler*, is hot on their trail, armed with heavy military equipment and backed by a shadowy private corporation.

As the GM, your role is to cultivate a sense of wonder, physical peril, and rapid-fire pacing. An adventure game must feel active: rope bridges should snap, ancient traps should activate on a misstep, and the jungle itself should feel like a living, breathing adversary. This book is written entirely in natural language to help you master these thematic elements, the action-movie style pacing, and the mechanical frameworks of each act, allowing you to react dynamically to your players' creative solutions while maintaining a high-quality, deeply memorable story.

## Act I: The Jungle Frontier

### Scene 1: Arriving at Puerto Maldonado
The adventure begins in the sweltering, chaotic border town of Puerto Maldonado, Peru—the last outpost of civilization before the dense, untamed green wall of the Amazon rainforest begins.

**Narrative Tone:**
The air in Puerto Maldonado is thick, humid, and smells of woodsmoke, rotting fruit, and river mud. A chaotic mix of riverboats, supply trucks, and pack mules clutters the dirt streets. Rain falls in sudden, warm torrents, turning the roads into rivers of red clay. In the taverns along the riverfront, fortune-hunters, rubber-tappers, and native guides share whispers of golden statues and lost expeditions that went into the green and never returned.

**Key NPCs in Puerto Maldonado:**
1. **Capitán Mateo Silva:** A rugged, cynical riverboat pilot who owns the *La Adelita*, a rusted wood-burning paddleboat. He is the only pilot brave (or crazy) enough to navigate the treacherous rapids of the Upper Madre de Dios River. He demands a high price for his services but is fiercely loyal once his word is given.
2. **Isabela Perez:** A local scholar who has spent years translating the regional oral histories. She can warn the characters about the *Guardians of the Canopy*—a legendary, isolated tribe said to guard the borders of Manóa with poisoned darts and ancient wooden traps.
3. **Klaus von Adler (Vanguard Group Commander):** A tall, scarred German mercenary who wears a crisp khaki military uniform. He is ruthless, highly disciplined, and believes that the gold of El Dorado belongs in the vaults of his corporate sponsors to fund their global expansion.

**Lore and Gathering Information (Social/Investigation):**
- *DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion/Intimidation):* The Vanguard Group has already hired fifty local laborers and purchased three heavy transport trucks, preparing to move into the jungle within twenty-four hours.
- *DC 14 Intelligence (History):* The Chronicles of Orellana mention a landmark known as the "Gates of Jaguar"—two massive stone pillars that mark the entrance to the sacred valley where Manóa lies.
- *DC 13 Wisdom (Survival):* The rainy season has begun early; the river currents are twice as fast as normal, and the low-lying bogs are teeming with swarms of disease-carrying blackflies.

### Scene 2: The River Run
The party boards *La Adelita* to travel deep into the interior. The river is a brown, churning serpent, lined with towering banks of green vines and skeletal kapok trees.

**The Encounter: Vanguard Pursuit**
While navigating the *Gorge of the Serpent*, a narrow canyon where the river narrows and speeds up, the party is attacked by two Vanguard motorized patrol boats.

*Tactics and Mechanics:*
- The Vanguard mercenaries are armed with rifles and throwing grenades, attempting to board *La Adelita* or disable its paddle wheel.
- *Riverboat Controls:* The characters can take control of the *La Adelita*’s mounted steam hose (which can spray blinding hot steam in a 15-foot cone) or use the ship’s cargo crane to swing heavy supply crates into the pursuing boats.
- *The Rapid Hazard:* The pilot of the boat must make a DC 13 Dexterity (Vehicles) check each round to avoid hitting submerged rocks or falling into whirlpools. If the boat takes 30 points of damage, its boiler begins to leak, requiring an emergency repair check (DC 14 Strength or Technology) before it explodes.

## Act II: The Green Abyss

### Scene 1: Navigating the Canopy
With their riverboat wrecked or left behind at the headwaters, the party must march on foot through the dense, uncharted rainforest. The canopy is so thick that it blocks out the sun, plunging the jungle floor into a perpetual, dim green twilight.

**Jungle Survival Subsystem (The Daily March):**
For each day of travel through the deep jungle, the party’s navigator must make a DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) check to keep the correct heading.
- *Success:* The party makes good progress and finds a secure, elevated campsite.
- *Failure:* The party is lost. They must roll on the *Jungle Hazards Table* and suffer 1 level of exhaustion due to heat and insects unless they spend an extra half-day recovering.

**Jungle Hazards Table (Roll 1d6):**
1. **Swarms of Bullet Ants:** The party stumbles into a nest. Every character must make a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or take 2d6 poison damage and have their movement speed halved for twenty-four hours due to intense, burning pain.
2. **The Rotten Bridge:** The path crosses a deep, 50-foot ravine bridged only by a decayed trunk of an ironwood tree covered in slippery moss. Crossing requires a DC 13 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check; on a failure, the character slips, requiring a dramatic rescue from their companions who hold the safety ropes.
3. **Vanguard Patrol:** A squad of four Vanguard soldiers, accompanied by two trained hunting mastiffs, is scouting the area. The party must sneak past (DC 14 Stealth) or engage in a quick, silent ambush before they can radio their main camp.
4. **Methane Gas Pocket:** The party enters a low hollow where decaying vegetation has trapped highly flammable pocket of methane. Any open flame (like a torch) or kinetic spark triggers an explosion, dealing 4d6 fire damage to everyone in a 15-foot radius.
5. **The Spiked Pit:** An ancient, overgrown pit-trap built by the jungle guardians. The lead character must make a DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check to notice the false floor of woven palm leaves. On a failure, they fall into the 15-foot pit, taking 2d6 piercing damage from the fire-hardened bamboo stakes at the bottom.
6. **Sudden Flash Flood:** A torrential cloudburst causes a nearby creek to erupt into a wall of muddy water. The party must climb into the canopy trees (DC 12 Strength (Athletics)) to avoid being swept away and losing half their supply gear.

## Act III: The Gates of Jaguar

### Scene 1: The Mayan Sentinels
The party arrives at the *Gates of Jaguar*—two towering basalt pillars carved in the likeness of crouching jaguars, marking the entrance to the sacred valley of Manóa.

**The Stand-off:**
The gates are guarded by the *Guardians of the Canopy*, led by their fierce war-chief, *Xibalba*. They are armed with obsidian-bladed spears, blowguns with paralyzing poison, and are highly skilled in forest camouflage.

*The Social Challenge:*
Xibalba does not immediately attack. He views the characters as greedy defilers, similar to the Vanguard Group.
- To earn passage, the party must negotiate with Xibalba. A character can attempt a DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion or History) check, presenting the *Chronicles of Orellana* as proof of their respect for their ancestors' history rather than a desire to steal the gold.
- Alternatively, the party can offer to help the guardians rescue several of their youth who were captured by the Vanguard Group to act as forced laborers for their excavation site.
- If they fail the negotiation, Xibalba demands a trial by combat: the party’s strongest warrior must face Xibalba’s champion in a hand-to-hand duel on a raised stone platform surrounded by sharpened stakes.

### Scene 2: The Vanguard Excavation
The Vanguard Group has established a massive military camp around the entrance to the golden city's primary temple vault. They have brought in heavy diesel generators, industrial drilling rigs, and have constructed wooden watchtowers equipped with searchlights.

**The Tactical Setup:**
- The camp is heavily guarded. Klaus von Adler has placed sniper nests on the surrounding cliff faces.
- The entrance to the vault is blocked by a massive, circular stone door, which Vanguard engineers are preparing to blow open using boxes of dynamite.
- The captured guardian youth are being forced to carry fuel and dynamite boxes under the watchful eyes of armed guards.

**The Assault/Infiltration:**
The party must coordinate a plan to stop the demolition.
- *The Stealth Route:* The characters can sneak into the fuel depot and sabotage the diesel generators, plunging the camp into darkness and disabling the searchlights.
- *The Guardian Alliance:* If the party allied with Xibalba, they can launch a coordinated attack, with the guardians using smoke-bombs and forest-fire arrows to create chaos while the characters target Von Adler’s command tent.
- *The Dynamite Counter:* A character can attempt to shoot the dynamite boxes from a distance, triggering a chain reaction that collapses the Vanguard's watchtowers and clears the temple entrance, but permanently sealing several side chambers of the vault.

## Act IV: The Vault of Manóa

### Scene 1: Entering the Golden City
Once inside the temple, the modern world disappears. The interior is a breathtaking complex of golden-leafed walls, silver-inlaid floors, and massive statues of ancient deities carved from solid jade. The air is dry and dusty, smelling of ancient copper and dry rot.

### Scene 2: The Chamber of the Sun
The central chamber is dominated by a massive, circular golden calendar-disk floating in the center of a deep, oil-filled pit. This is the *Chamber of the Sun*.

**The Puzzle: The Solar Alignment**
To open the inner sanctum where the primary treasure of El Dorado lies, the characters must align three concentric stone rings on the ceiling representing the Moon, the Sun, and the Earth.
- Around the room are three stone cranks. Turning a crank rotates one of the rings.
- *The Clues (Carved on the walls in Mayan hieroglyphs):*
  1. "The Moon must reflect the light of the Sun, and therefore sits at the right hand of the Creator."
  2. "The Earth lies in eternal shadow, placed directly opposite the blinding eye of the solar deity."
  3. "When the alignment is true, the silver beam of the moon shall strike the jade heart of the calendar-disk."
- *The Mechanical Hazard:*
  - If the cranks are turned in the incorrect sequence, the oil in the pit is ignited by a flint-and-steel mechanism, sending a wall of fire rushing through the room. The characters must solve the puzzle while dodging flares of burning oil (dealing 3d6 fire damage to anyone within 5 feet).

### Scene 3: The Guardian of the Vault
The final guardian of the golden city is a mechanical marvel: the *Obsidian Colossus*—a 15-foot-tall automaton constructed of polished black stone, gold leaf, and powered by a highly advanced clockwork steam engine.

*Combat Tactics and Weaknesses:*
- The Colossus is nearly immune to ordinary physical attacks due to its thick stone plating.
- *Steam Venting:* Every 2 rounds, the Colossus must vent excess steam from its shoulder ports. During this turn, its AC is reduced by 4, and its vulnerable copper gears are exposed.
- *Water Weakness:* If the characters can rupture one of the water pipes lining the chamber walls, spraying cold water onto the heated obsidian plating of the Colossus, the sudden thermal shock cracks its stone armor, making it vulnerable to kinetic damage.

## Act V: The Temple Collapse

### Scene 1: The Final Showdown with Von Adler
Just as the party defeats the Colossus and claims the *Scepter of the Jaguar King*—the solid gold, emerald-eyed artifact that acts as the key to El Dorado's treasures—Klaus von Adler emerges from the shadows. He is bloodied, desperate, and holds a detonator connected to dynamite blocks placed around the temple's support pillars.

**The Final Battle:**
Von Adler fights with a heavy submachine gun, utilizing the crumbling stone pillars for cover. He is accompanied by his remaining elite bodyguards.
*The Dynamic Environment:*
- At the start of each round, the ceiling cracks. Small stones fall, requiring characters to make a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw to avoid 1d6 bludgeoning damage.
- *The Detonator:* If Von Adler is reduced to half HP, he threatens to press the detonator, burying everyone alive. A character can attempt a targeted ranged attack to shoot the detonator out of his hand, or use a quick grapple check (DC 14 Strength) to wrest it away.

### Scene 2: The Escape Sequence (Action-Movie Style)
When Von Adler is defeated or the dynamite is triggered, the temple begins to collapse in earnest. The players must navigate a series of rapid hazards to escape back to the jungle floor.

- **The Falling Pillar:** A massive basalt pillar collapses across their exit route. A character must use their strength (DC 14 Athletics) to lift the stone, or use an explosive charge to shatter it while other players slip beneath.
- **The Chasm Leap:** The stone floor splits, creating a 15-foot-deep chasm that is rapidly filling with toxic volcanic gas. The characters must leap across (DC 13 Strength (Athletics)) or use a whip or climbing rope to swing across the gap.
- **The Sinking City:** As they emerge from the temple, they witness the entire valley floor sinking into a massive, underground water reservoir. The golden structures of Manóa are sliding into the lake, creating massive whirlpools. The characters must run up the crumbling stone stairs of the valley wall (DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics)) to reach the safety of the high cliffs.

### Scene 3: Epilogue
The characters stand on the edge of the cliff, bruised and exhausted, looking down at the empty, water-filled lake basin where Manóa once stood. The golden city of El Dorado has been buried forever beneath the waters of the Amazon.

**The Final Resolution:**
The characters have survived and managed to escape with only a few relics, including the *Scepter of the Jaguar King*. They must decide:
1. **Deliver the Scepter to a Museum:** They will receive global academic acclaim and secure funding for their next expedition, but the scepter will be kept behind glass, far from the people who built it.
2. **Return the Scepter to Xibalba’s Tribe:** They will earn the eternal gratitude of the Guardians of the Canopy, who will guide them safely back to civilization. The scepter remains in the jungle, preserving the sacred heritage of the valley.
3. **Sell the Scepter on the Black Market:** They can sell the artifact to a private collector in New York for an immense fortune (50,000 dollars), allowing them to buy their own ships and planes, but ruining their academic reputations and making them targets for international law enforcement.

## Act VI: The Golden Pantheon, Lost Tribes, and Hidden Ruins

To expand this pulp adventure into a multi-part campaign or a sandbox world, a Game Master should have access to a rich mythological framework, additional factions, and modular side locations.

### The Mythology of the Sun King

The city of Manóa was built by a breakaway branch of the Mayan civilization that migrated south into the Amazon Basin around 900 AD. They worshiped the *Sun King, Inti-Ra*, a solar deity who represented life, purification, and the destructive power of heat.

- **The Golden Code:** The worshipers of Inti-Ra believed that gold was the physical sweat of the Sun King. It was not used as currency, but as a sacred shielding material to protect their temples from the dark forces of the underworld, known as *Xibalba* (the Place of Fear).
- **The Obsidian Guard:** These were elite warriors who bound their eyes with thin strips of polished obsidian. They trained to fight entirely by sound and wind-currents, believing that by blinding themselves to the physical world, they could see the spiritual light of the Sun King.
- **The Prophecy of the Shattered Disk:** According to legend, when a "pale star from the east" enters the Chamber of the Sun and aligns the calendars with selfish intent, the foundations of the world will crack, and the golden city will be reclaimed by the primeval waters.

## Act VII: Extended Sector Locations

The Amazon Basin is vast and holds many secrets. If the party chooses to explore other sectors before reaching the main valley of Manóa, they can navigate to these locations.

### 1. The Lost Aviary of the Harpy Kings
A ruined stone amphitheater built atop a high cliff, covered in colorful tropical orchids. It is the nesting ground for thousands of exotic birds, including giant, mutated *Scythian Harpy Eagles*.
- **The Challenge:** The aviary is guarded by a colony of eagles that are highly protective of their young. In the center of the ruins lies the skeleton of a Spanish conquistador, clutching an ivory scroll-case that contains a map of the valley's underground waterways.
- **The Solution:** The characters can use bird-calls (DC 13 Performance) to calm the eagles, or use smoke-bombs to temporarily blind them while they retrieve the scroll-case.

### 2. The Sunken Mission of San Ignacio
A partially submerged Jesuit mission from the 17th century, choked with water lilies and guarded by giant anacondas. The church bells still ring occasionally when the river currents are strong.
- **The Challenge:** The mission’s flooded crypt contains a solid silver altar piece and a chest of historical letters that reveal the true fate of the Spanish expeditions that sought El Dorado.
- **The Solution:** The characters must dive into the flooded crypt (requiring a DC 13 Athletics check) while dodging the territorial giant anacondas that nest in the rotted pews.

## Act IX: Advanced Exploration and Trap Design

To elevate your campaign and make the players feel like true tomb raiders, a Game Master should employ intricate traps and environmental challenges. This section details advanced exploration mechanics and custom traps designed specifically for the ancient vaults of Manóa.

### Traps of the Golden Pantheon

The ancient Mayan builders of Manóa were master engineers. Their traps were not merely lethal devices; they were ceremonial trials meant to test the physical prowess, coordination, and spiritual worth of those who entered.

#### 1. The Pendulum of Xibalba (Mechanical Trap)
A massive, obsidian blade shaped like a crescent moon, swinging silently across a narrow, 10-foot-wide stone bridge overlooking a deep pit of spikes.

- **Trigger:** A pressure plate hidden in the center of the bridge. Stepping on the plate releases the latch holding the counterweight, launching the blade across the corridor.
- **Deduction and Detection:**
  - A character can detect the pressure plate with a DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation) check. The plate is slightly lower than the surrounding stones and is marked with a carving of a crescent moon.
- **Deactivation/Bypass:**
  - *The Mechanical Solution:* A character can jam the pressure plate with a iron spike or wedge a heavy stone beneath it. This requires a DC 13 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check.
  - *The Timing Solution:* A character can study the swing pattern of the blade. With a successful DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) check, they can identify a 2-second window when the blade reaches its apex and stops. They can then leap across the bridge (DC 13 Dexterity (Acrobatics)) during this window.
- **Consequences of Failure:**
  - If triggered, the blade sweeps across the bridge. Every creature on the bridge must make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, they take 4d10 slashing damage and are knocked off the bridge into the spike pit below (taking an additional 2d6 piercing damage). On a success, they drop prone on the bridge, taking no damage.

#### 2. The Trial of the Gilded Scales (Weight/Balance Trap)
A circular chamber where the floor is a massive, balanced stone disc floating atop a central stone spindle. Walking too far to one side causes the floor to tilt, sending characters sliding into the oil-filled pit that surrounds the room.

- **Trigger:** Any movement across the chamber that is not balanced. The room's entry and exit doors are on opposite sides of the floating disc.
- **The Mechanics:**
  - The disc has a diameter of 40 feet. If the total weight on one half of the disc exceeds the weight on the other half by more than 150 pounds, the disc tilts at a 30-degree angle.
  - *Tilt Check:* Every character on the tilting side must make a DC 13 Dexterity (Acrobatics) saving throw to keep their footing. On a failure, they slide 15 feet toward the oil pit.
- **The Solution:**
  - The characters must coordinate their movements. They can divide into two teams of equal weight and move along opposite edges of the disc in sync, keeping the floor perfectly level. This requires a DC 12 Wisdom (Insight/Cooperation) check or active communication.
  - Alternatively, they can secure a rope to the entry portal and use a grappling hook to secure the other end to the exit portal, creating a safety line they can slide across (DC 14 Strength (Athletics)).

Use these mechanical traps to add suspense and physical problem-solving to your dungeon crawls!
