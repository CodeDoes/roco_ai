# The Shadow of the Sunken Spire: A Comprehensive Fantasy Adventure Guide

## Introduction and System Setup

Welcome, Game Master, to *The Shadow of the Sunken Spire*, an epic, high-stakes fantasy adventure designed for a party of 4 to 6 players starting at 3rd level and progressing to 6th level. This book serves as a robust, non-linear blueprint for your campaign, giving you all the tools, lore, narrative guidance, encounter rules, and character matrices you need to run a flawless and deeply immersive experience.

In this adventure, the characters will venture into the Shadowed Reach, a region of ancient forests, cursed ruins, and forgotten history. At the heart of this region lies the Sunken Spire—a subterranean wizard's laboratory and vault that was swallowed by the earth during the catastrophic *Shattering of the Weave* four centuries ago. Recently, seismic activities and a surge of wild magic have begun tearing the earth open, revealing the spire's crown and unleashing a corrupting plague of crystal and ash upon the land.

As the GM, your role is not just to recite text, but to act as a dynamic arbitrator, environmental storyteller, and narrative guide. This book is written specifically in natural language to help you internalize the themes, the pacing, and the key mechanics of each act, allowing you to react organically to your players' choices while maintaining a cohesive, high-quality, and deeply memorable story.

## Act I: The Blighted Frontier

### Scene 1: Arrival at Oakhaven
The adventure begins in the border village of Oakhaven, a struggling settlement nestled at the edge of the Whispering Woods. Oakhaven was once a bustling logging town, but the encroaching blighted crystal formation has poisoned the nearby rivers and driven the woodland beasts into a crazed frenzy.

**Narrative Tone:**
The air in Oakhaven is heavy, smelling of damp earth and the sharp, metallic tang of ozone. The buildings are weather-worn, their timber frames stained grey. The villagers walk with hunched shoulders, cast in the long shadows of the towering pines. In the center of the village stands a massive, dead oak tree, its ancient branches partially petrified into jagged, translucent red crystal that pulses with a faint, ominous light.

**Key NPCs in Oakhaven:**
1. **Mayor Corin Vance:** A tired, greying human who wears his heavy bronze chain of office over a patched wool coat. He is desperate to save his people but has run out of options. He believes the blight is a divine curse, but his rational mind suspects something ancient and forgotten.
2. **Kaelen the Herbalist:** A blind elf who can smell the exact nature of rot and sickness. Kaelen can provide the characters with a recipe for a temporary balm that offers resistance to the crystalline rot, but they must retrieve *Moondew Petals* from the infected bogs to craft it.
3. **Barnd the Barkeep (The Whispering Tavern):** A robust dwarf who has heard whispers from hunters about glowing stone ruins emerging from the earth. He is nervous, constantly wiping down clean cups, and knows that local bandits have begun worshipping the crystalline shards.

**Lore & Information Gathered (Social/Investigation):**
- *DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion/Intimidation) or Intelligence (History):* The red crystal is known as "Sanguine Quartz." It grows rapidly on dead organic matter and seems to absorb and corrupt ambient magical energy.
- *DC 15 Wisdom (Insight):* Mayor Vance is hiding the fact that his own daughter, Elara, has contracted the crystalline rot and is currently locked in their basement, slowly turning to stone.
- *DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana):* Sanguine Quartz is not natural; it matches historical descriptions of magical focal points used by the Archmage Morvath, who perished during the Shattering of the Weave.

### Scene 2: The Forest Path and the Crystalline Ambush
As the party ventures into the Whispering Woods toward the source of the corruption, the environment shifts. The green of the forest is replaced by ash-white bark, pale moss, and needles of sharp red crystal growing from the roots of ancient trees.

**The Encounter:**
The party is ambushed by a pack of *Blight-Crazed Wolves*. These beasts have shards of Sanguine Quartz protruding from their spines and eye sockets, their jaws dripping with a viscous, glowing saliva.

*Tactics and Mechanics:*
- The wolves do not fight like normal animals; they are fearless and coordinated, guided by a singular, hive-like magical frequency.
- *Sanguine Bite:* If a character is bitten by a wolf, they must succeed on a DC 11 Constitution saving throw or suffer 1d4 necrotic damage and become *Blight-Marked* for 1 hour. While Blight-Marked, they radiate a dim red light, and other corrupted creatures have advantage on attack rolls against them.
- *Environmental Hazard:* Jagged crystal formations line the forest floor. Any creature shoved or knocked prone onto these formations takes 1d6 piercing damage and must make the save against Blight-Marking.

### Scene 3: The Hermit's Sanctuary
Deep within the forest, untouched by the rot, is a small glade protected by an ancient stone circle. Here lives *Seraphina*, an old druid who has kept watch over the region for decades.

**Roleplay Guidance:**
Seraphina is eccentric but highly alert. She speaks in riddles but possesses crucial knowledge about the Sunken Spire. She reveals that the spire was Morvath’s central laboratory, and that it contains an artifact known as the *Heart of Eternity*—the focus point that Morvath used to draw energy from the Weave. The spire is rising because the magical wards that held it subterranean are failing, and the decaying artifact is leaking pure, corrupted energy.

**The Choice:**
Seraphina offers the party a choice:
- **The Path of Warding:** She can give them a ritual to suppress the crystalline growth temporarily, making their trek through the bogs much safer but alerting the cultists who reside there.
- **The Path of Shadows:** She can teach them a secret route through the caves, bypassing the cultists entirely but forcing them to face the subterranean horrors that nest in the dark.

## Act II: The Weeping Bogs and the Cult of the Red Star

### Scene 1: Navigating the Bogs
The Weeping Bogs are a treacherous expanse of black mud, toxic gas, and half-submerged ruins. The air is thick with mist, making visibility extremely poor (maximum 30 feet).

**Hazards and Survival Mechanics:**
- *Toxic Miasma:* Every hour spent traveling through the deep bogs requires a DC 12 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, a character gains one level of exhaustion due to inhaling the sulfurous, crystal-tainted gas. Characters wearing wet cloths over their mouths have advantage on this save.
- *Quick-Mud:* A character leading the party must make a DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) check. On a failure, the party stumbles into deep quick-mud. The lead character must make a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) saving throw to avoid being pulled under, requiring active rescue from their companions.

### Scene 2: The Temple of the Red Star
At the center of the bogs, built upon the ruins of an ancient temple, is the camp of the *Cult of the Red Star*. Led by a fanatical tiefling named *Malakor*, the cult believes the rising spire is a celestial herald of a new age of blood and stone.

**The Cult's Force and Setup:**
- The camp is heavily fortified with spiked wooden palisades and watchtowers.
- Cultists patrol in pairs, accompanied by *Crystalline Beetles*—hand-sized constructs that chirp loudly when they detect living tissue.
- **Malakor:** A charismatic, power-hungry warlock who wields a staff topped with a flawless, pulsing Sanguine Quartz. He can channel the energy of the spire to cast destructive fire and necrotic spells.

**Infiltration and Assault Options:**
- *Stealth:* Characters can slip through the swamp-grass under the cover of night. A DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) group check is required. If they succeed, they can poison the cult's food supplies or free several captured villagers from Oakhaven who are scheduled for sacrifice.
- *Frontal Assault:* If the party chooses to fight, they can use the marsh terrain to their advantage, collapsing watchtowers onto cultists or using the toxic gas pockets (which explode when exposed to open flame) to clear groups of enemies.
- *Deception:* Using stolen cultist robes, a character can attempt a DC 15 Charisma (Deception) check to convince the guards they are recruits from a distant cell.

**The Confrontation with Malakor:**
Malakor fights from an elevated stone altar where he is performing a ritual to hasten the spire's rise.
*Special Mechanic: The Sanguine Link.*
- During combat, Malakor is shielded by a barrier of red light that makes him immune to all damage. This barrier is powered by three large crystal shards placed around the altar.
- To harm Malakor, the characters must destroy these shards (AC 12, 15 HP each) or redirect the flow of magic using an Intelligence (Arcana) check (DC 14) to overload them.
- *Malakor’s Death Throes:* When defeated, Malakor's body turns entirely to Sanguine Quartz and shatters, releasing a wave of concussive force that knocks everyone back and triggers an earthquake. This earthquake splits the ground, revealing the massive, obsidian crown of the Sunken Spire rising directly beneath the temple ruins.

## Act III: The Sunken Spire

### Scene 1: The Descent
The party must climb down into the yawning chasm created by the earthquake. The walls of the chasm are lined with shattered masonry, twisted tree roots, and glowing veins of Sanguine Quartz. The spire itself is tilted at a twenty-degree angle, wedged between massive stone pillars.

**The Spire’s Atmosphere:**
The interior of the spire is dead silent, save for the constant, low-frequency hum of magical energy that vibrates in the characters' teeth. Dust motes float in the air, drifting toward the center of the structure. Gravity is slightly distorted here due to the damaged magical wards; characters can leap twice as far as normal, but falling distances are deceptive.

### Scene 2: Chamber 1 – The Library of Forgotten Wills
This vast, circular room is lined with towering bookshelves. Many of the books have rotted into dust, but several floating, glowing scrolls remain intact. The floor is covered in a shallow pool of black, ink-like water.

**The Puzzle: The Constellation of the Weave**
To open the heavy adamantine door leading deeper into the spire, the characters must solve the puzzle of the constellation.
- On the ceiling, a map of the night sky is painted in glowing silver ink. Four key stars are dim and empty.
- In the room, four statues of ancient wizards stand, each holding a different colored lens (Red, Blue, Green, Yellow).
- The characters must align the lenses so that the ambient light reflecting off the ink-water passes through the lenses and hits the correct stars on the ceiling.
- *Clues:*
  1. "The star of flame burns red, positioned at the zenith of the northern sky."
  2. "The star of the deep blue sea must never gaze upon the star of flame."
  3. "The star of growth, green as spring, sits adjacent to the blue star, basking in its shadow."
  4. "The star of light, golden yellow, sits directly opposite the red star, balancing the cosmic scales."
- *Consequences of Failure:* If an incorrect combination is attempted, the ink-water rises, and two *Inky Water Weirds* emerge to attack. The characters must defeat them before they can try the puzzle again.

### Scene 3: Chamber 2 – The Laboratory of Flesh and Stone
This room is filled with iron tables, glass vats filled with green preservation fluid, and rusted surgical tools. Here, Morvath conducted experiments to fuse living creatures with magical crystals.

**The Monstrosity: The Sanguine Golem**
Guardians of this chamber are a *Sanguine Golem*—a towering construct made of compressed stone and raw Sanguine Quartz—and several *Rot-Flies* that nest in its hollow chest cavity.

*Tactics and Weaknesses:*
- The golem is slow but incredibly strong. Its attacks can shatter stone and bone.
- *Crystalline Overcharge:* If the golem takes lightning or fire damage, its crystal components glow intensely. On its next turn, it can release a 15-foot cone of sharp crystal shards, dealing heavy damage to anyone caught in the area.
- *Shatter-Point:* A character who succeeds on a DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Perception) check can identify a structural flaw in the golem's right knee. Attacking this shatter-point with bludgeoning damage has advantage and, on a hit, knocks the golem prone.

## Act IV: The Heart of Eternity

### Scene 1: The Sanctum of Morvath
The lowest level of the spire is a massive dome of polished black marble. In the center of the room, floating above a deep, bottomless pit, is the *Heart of Eternity*—a massive, multi-faceted diamond the size of a wagon, pulsing with an intense, blinding red light. Arcane lightning arcs from the crystal to the walls of the dome, cracking the marble and causing the entire structure to shake.

**Morvath’s Echo:**
Standing before the crystal is the spectral remnant of *Archmage Morvath*. He is not a physical being, but a hollow projection of his final, mad thoughts, bound to the decaying artifact.

**The Final Encounter: Phase 1 – The Spectral Mage**
Morvath’s spectral form attacks the party, wielding powerful evocation and illusion magic.
*Combat Mechanics:*
- Morvath can teleport across the chamber as a bonus action, leaving behind a mirror image of himself.
- He can summon *Spectral Blades* that fly through the air and attack characters independently.
- *The Unstable Floor:* On initiative count 20 of each round, a portion of the marble floor crumbles into the pit. The GM should mark which tiles are unstable at the start of the round, giving players a chance to move before they collapse.

**Phase 2 – The Heart’s Awakening**
When Morvath’s spectral form is reduced to 0 HP, his energy is absorbed back into the Heart of Eternity. The crystal turns dark purple, and four massive, crystalline legs erupt from its base. The artifact itself is now a living, rampaging entity of raw, corrupt magic.

*Tactics and Mechanics:*
- *Arcane Pulse:* At the start of its turn, the Heart releases a pulse of raw magic. Every character must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be pushed 10 feet back (potentially into the crumbling pit).
- *Vulnerability:* The Heart's soft, inner core is exposed only immediately after it fires its primary weapon, the *Disintegration Ray*. A character can ready an action to strike the core when it opens, dealing double damage.
- *Healing the Blight:* If a character uses an item or spell that purifies or cures disease (such as *Lesser Restoration* or Kaelen’s herbal balm) directly on one of the Heart's crystalline legs, it disables that leg for 1 round, reducing its movement speed and disabling its stomp attack.

## Act V: Resolution and Epilogue

### Scene 1: The Choice at the Brink
Once the Heart of Eternity is defeated, it cracks open, revealing a small, pure blue seed of pristine, uncorrupted magical energy—the *Core of the Weave*. The spire is rapidly collapsing, and the party has only minutes to escape before the chasm closes over them.

The characters must make a critical decision regarding the Core:
1. **Destroy the Core:** This will completely purge the Sanguine Quartz blight from the region forever, but it will also strip all magic from the Whispering Woods, rendering it a mundane, silent forest and turning all magic-using denizens (including Seraphina) into ordinary, mortal beings.
2. **Purify and Claim the Core:** A character can attempt a difficult ritual to absorb the core’s pure energy. This requires a series of three DC 15 checks (Arcana, Religion, and Constitution). On a success, they become a living conduit of the purified Weave, gaining powerful magical abilities but marking them as a target for every power-hungry wizard and cult in the continent. On a failure, they are consumed by the magic, their body scattering into harmless, glittering dust.
3. **Seal the Spire:** They can leave the core inside the spire and use their combined strength to trigger a massive collapse, burying the artifact deep beneath the earth once more. The blight will remain as a slow, manageable scar on the land, but the secret of the spire will be preserved, waiting for future generations.

### Scene 2: Return to Oakhaven
The party returns to Oakhaven to witness the consequences of their choice.
- If they destroyed the core, the red crystals on the dead oak tree crumble into sand. Mayor Vance’s daughter is cured of her stone affliction, but the air is cold and empty of the magical spark that once made the forest alive.
- If they claimed or purified the core, Oakhaven begins to prosper, its soil becoming incredibly fertile, but strange, cloaked figures are spotted watching the village from the tree line, searching for the new bearer of the Weave.
- If they sealed the spire, the village remains in a state of cautious peace, ever-watchful of the earth, knowing that what lies beneath can never truly be forgotten.

## Act VI: Expanded World-Building, Factions, and Deep Lore

To truly bring the Shadowed Reach to life, a Game Master must have a deep well of lore, history, and structural sociology to draw from. This section expands the regional factions, details local geographic anomalies, and provides additional sub-quests to weave into the main narrative fabric.

### The Geography of the Shadowed Reach

The Shadowed Reach is a geographically isolated basin rimmed by the Razor Ridge Mountains to the north and east, and bounded by the bottomless maw of the Blackwood Chasm to the south. This isolation has allowed unique ecological and magical niches to develop undisturbed for centuries.

1. **The Whispering Woods:**
   An ancient canopy of sentinel pines, ironwood, and weeping willows. The forest gets its name from a peculiar acoustic property: the wind rustling through the leaves carries a low-frequency hum that sounds remarkably like hushed human speech. Travelers often lose their minds here, believing the trees are whispering their deepest secrets or mocking their failures.
   - *Magical Phenomenon (The Sounding Wind):* Any character who rests in the woods without plugging their ears with wax or casting a ward must make a DC 11 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, their sleep is plagued by nightmares of their past, and they do not regain hit dice during that long rest.

2. **The Weeping Bogs:**
   A sunken wetland fed by the silt-heavy waters of the Greyflow River. The bogs are a graveyard of empires; beneath the muck lie the fossilized bones of prehistoric beasts and the waterlogged foundations of elven watchtowers from the First Age. The water is highly acidic, capable of slowly eating away at non-magical iron and leather over extended exposure.
   - *Acidic Decay:* Any non-magical steel weapon or armor immersed in the bog water for more than an hour suffers a permanent and cumulative -1 penalty to damage rolls or AC (to a maximum of -3) unless treated with animal fat or magical oil.

3. **The Razor Ridge Mountains:**
   These jagged, snow-capped peaks are practically impassable, save for the Dead Man's Pass, a treacherous road guarded by the abandoned fortress of Ironfang Keep. The mountains are rich in minerals, including rare veins of *Star-Metal* (orichalcum), which can be forged into weapons capable of bypassing the damage resistances of constructs and aberrations.

### Comprehensive Faction Profiles

The political landscape of the Shadowed Reach is a delicate web of tension, ready to snap at the slightest provocation. There are three primary factions operating in this region, each with its own agenda regarding the Sunken Spire.

#### 1. The Circle of the Iron Root
A conservative, isolationist circle of druids, rangers, and forest-folk who believe that the natural order must be preserved at all costs—even if it means the death of Oakhaven.

- **Leader:** *Arch-Druid Seraphina* (detailed in Act I).
- **Core Beliefs:** Magic is a wild, dangerous river. Civilization is a dam that seeks to control it, which inevitably leads to catastrophic bursts like the Shattering of the Weave. They believe the Sanguine Quartz is nature's immune response, a scab forming over the deep magical wound left by Morvath.
- **Resource Base:** They control the secret paths of the Whispering Woods, utilize trained beasts (such as giant owls and shadow mastiffs) for reconnaissance, and possess ancient herbal remedies that can neutralize almost any poison.
- **Attitude Toward the Party:**
  - *Friendly:* If the party respects the forest, avoids fire magic, and seeks to seal the spire without exploiting its power.
  - *Hostile:* If the party uses destructive magic, allies with the cultists, or expresses a desire to harness the Core of the Weave for personal glory.

#### 2. The Consortium of the Brass Cog
A syndicate of merchants, arcanists, and excavators from the southern metropolises. They view the rising of the Sunken Spire not as a disaster, but as the ultimate business opportunity.

- **Leader:** *Baroness Vespera Vance* (a distant cousin of Mayor Corin Vance, though she despises him). She is an elegant woman who wears velvet gowns modified with clockwork brass braces that assist her mobility.
- **Core Beliefs:** Knowledge and technology are the only true paths to human advancement. The treasures of the Sunken Spire belong in academic vaults and museums, where they can be analyzed, replicated, and sold to the highest bidder.
- **Resource Base:** A private army of highly disciplined mercenaries, armed with experimental heavy crossbows and alchemical explosives. They also employ low-level wizards to map magical anomalies.
- **Attitude Toward the Party:**
  - *Friendly:* If the party is willing to act as freelance recovery agents, bringing back specific blueprints, research journals, and magical relics from inside the spire.
  - *Hostile:* If the party attempts to destroy the spire's contents or keeps the most valuable artifacts for themselves.

#### 3. The Cult of the Red Star
A fanatical, apocalyptic doomsday cult comprised of desperate outcasts, runaway serfs, and power-hungry hedge wizards who worship the Sanguine Quartz.

- **Leader:** *Malakor the Defiler* (detailed in Act II).
- **Core Beliefs:** The world is fundamentally corrupt and must be cleansed in fire and stone. They believe the Sanguine Quartz is the blood of a dead god, and that by fusing their bodies with the crystals, they will transcend mortality and inherit the ruined world.
- **Resource Base:** Swarms of fanatical cultists who fight without fear of death, corrupted woodland monsters, and dark ritual sites scattered throughout the Weeping Bogs.
- **Attitude Toward the Party:** Unconditionally hostile. They view the characters as blind sheep who seek to delay the inevitable rebirth of the world. However, Malakor may offer a temporary truce if he believes he can use the party to bypass the spire's entry wards.

## Act VII: Side Quests and Regional Events

To give your campaign more breathing room and allow the players to explore the world at their own pace, you can introduce these modular side quests and random environmental events.

### Sub-Quest A: The Lost Shipment
*Recommended for Level 3 parties before entering the Weeping Bogs.*

- **Hook:** Mayor Vance informs the party that a vital shipment of winter supplies and iron tools from the south is overdue. The wagon was last seen entering the Dead Man's Pass through the Razor Ridge Mountains.
- **The Investigation:**
  - The party travels to the pass and finds the wagon overturned, its draft horses slaughtered, and its cargo missing.
  - A DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) check reveals tracks of heavily armed humanoids leading into the nearby hills.
- **The Den:**
  - The tracks lead to an old bandit hideout built into the ruins of *Ironfang Keep*. The bandits are led by a ruthless half-orc named *Gharz the Red*, who has found a small cluster of Sanguine Quartz and is using it to enhance his weapons.
  - Gharz fights with a massive iron greataxe that has red crystals fused to the blade.
- **The Twist:**
  - Gharz did not attack the wagon for profit. He was hired by Baroness Vespera of the Brass Cog to intercept the shipment to pressure Mayor Vance into signing over the mineral rights of Oakhaven's surrounding forests.
- **Resolution:**
  - The party can defeat the bandits, retrieve the supplies, and secure Oakhaven's survival. They also find correspondence linking the Brass Cog to the bandits, giving them political leverage over Baroness Vespera.

### Sub-Quest B: The Whispering Tree's Lament
*Recommended for Level 4 parties while exploring the Whispering Woods.*

- **Hook:** Kaelen the Herbalist tells the party about the "Mother Tree," the oldest oak in the Whispering Woods, which is slowly being choked to death by a massive parasitic growth of Sanguine Quartz at its core.
- **The Challenge:**
  - The Mother Tree is surrounded by a field of intense magical resonance. Any spell cast within 100 feet of the tree triggers a wild magic surge.
  - The quartz parasite is guarded by a *Crystalline Dryad*—a tragic forest spirit whose wooden flesh has been partially replaced by glowing red crystal. She is mad with pain and attacks anyone who approaches the tree.
- **The Solution:**
  - The party can choose to kill the Dryad, which will save the tree but earn the eternal enmity of Seraphina's druids.
  - Alternatively, a character can attempt to surgically extract the crystal parasite without killing the Dryad. This requires three successful DC 14 Wisdom (Medicine) or Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) checks made in sequence while other party members hold the Dryad down or distract her without dealing lethal damage.
- **Reward:**
  - If successful, the Dryad is freed from her torment and rewards the party with the *Heart of the Forest*, a magical seed that can be planted to instantly purify a 1-mile area of any magical or physical corruption.

## Act IX: Running the Final Scene - A Step-by-Step DM Guide

The climax of this adventure is designed to be intense, chaotic, and cinematic. Here is a guide on how to structure the final encounter in Morvath's Sanctum to ensure it flows perfectly.

### Step 1: Setting the Stage
- Place the players on the outer edge of the circular room. The center contains the bottomless pit, over which the Heart of Eternity floats, tethered by three arcs of red lightning connected to the walls.
- Emphasize the sound: the deep, rumbling vibration of the spire, the cracking of marble, and the howling of wild magic wind.
- Place Morvath's Spectral Echo in the center of the room, standing on a translucent platform of solid force.

### Step 2: Phase 1 Management
- Morvath will use his mobility to stay out of reach of melee fighters, forcing them to use ranged attacks or find creative ways to reach him (such as leaping across the distorted gravity field).
- Encourage the players to target the tethering crystals on the walls. If they destroy a crystal, Morvath loses one of his legendary actions or has his AC reduced by 2.
- *The Unstable Floor:* Keep track of the tiles. At the end of each round, describe how cracks spread through a specific section of the floor. On the next round, that section crumbles into the abyss. This keeps the players constantly moving and prevents them from standing in one spot and repeating attacks.

### Step 3: The Transition
- When Morvath is defeated, do not immediately roll initiative for Phase 2. Pause for a narrative beat. Describe how his spectral form screams, dissolving into red mist that is sucked into the Heart of Eternity.
- The Heart begins to shake violently, its pure blue center turning a toxic crimson. The stone floor shakes, and the crystalline legs erupt.
- This is a great moment to describe the visual changes: the red light intensifies, casting long, dancing shadows across the crumbling walls. The air becomes hot and dry, smelling of copper and burning ozone.

### Step 4: Phase 2 Execution
- The Heart of Eternity is now a physical menace. It will attempt to stomp characters who are close or fire its disintegration ray at distant targets.
- *The Disintegration Ray:* This is a highly telegraphed attack. At the start of a round, state that the Heart is charging its weapon and point to a specific line across the map. On initiative count 10, the ray fires, dealing massive damage to anyone still standing in that line. This gives players an entire round to scramble out of the way.
- Maintain the pressure of the collapsing room. By the time the Heart is near death, the players should have only a few secure marble platforms left to stand on, creating a truly epic "on the brink of ruin" finale.
