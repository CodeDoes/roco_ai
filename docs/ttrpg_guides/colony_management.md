# Colony-9: The New Frontier - A Comprehensive Colony Management TTRPG Adventure Guide

## Introduction and System Setup

Welcome, Game Master, to *Colony-9: The New Frontier*, a massive, systemic colony-management and tabletop roleplaying campaign guide designed for a party of 4 to 6 players. This comprehensive book serves as a multi-layered blueprint for your campaign, introducing resource-tracking tables, political systems, engineering subgrids, trade matrices, and crisis survival loops.

The setting of this adventure is the hostile, terraformed world of *Aegis Prime*. The characters are the "Colonial Council"—the governing body of Colony-9, a newly established settlement situated in the fertile but treacherous valley of the *Mistcliffs*. They are responsible for the physical safety, economic growth, and psychological well-being of three hundred civilian colonists. Unlike a typical dungeon-crawl, the challenges in this campaign are systemic: food storage must survive the winter, water filtration grids must be maintained, and the delicate political balance between corporate miners, scientific idealists, and military security forces must be preserved.

As the GM, you represent the hostile elements of Aegis Prime: the extreme climate, the local alien fauna, and the cold reality of corporate resource allocations. This book is written entirely in natural language to help you master these systemic mechanics, the political factions, and the critical pacing of each colony phase, allowing you to run a highly satisfying, strategic, and deeply memorable campaign where every choice is measured in food, water, and human lives.

## Act I: Establishment and Construction

### Scene 1: Arrival and Site Selection
The campaign begins with the drop-ship *USG Vanguard* landing on the surface of Aegis Prime. The colonists emerge into a world of towering red rock formations, toxic purple-tinted vegetation, and a heavy, ozone-scented atmosphere that requires low-grade respiratory filters.

**Narrative Tone:**
The air is cold, carrying a constant, biting wind off the Mistcliffs. The sky is a pale lavender, banded by the golden rings of the gas giant orbiting overhead. The primary landing site is a muddy clearing littered with titanium supply crates, half-constructed habitat domes, and the constant, high-pitched whine of diesel generators struggling to power the perimeter fences.

**Key Factions inside Colony-9:**
1. **The Union of Excavators (The Blue-Collars):** Led by *Gunnar "Iron" Vance*, a gruff, scarred mining veteran. They represent the labor force that runs the ore processors and builds the heavy infrastructure. They value physical safety, food rations, and fair wages.
2. **The New Dawn Association (The Idealists):** Led by *Dr. Aria Thorne*, a brilliant, soft-spoken botanist. They are scientists and terraforming specialists who believe Colony-9 should be a self-sustaining, eco-friendly utopia, free from corporate greed.
3. **The Aegis Security Force (The Militants):** Led by *Commander Marcus Sterling*, a stern, heavily armored veteran who views the local alien wildlife and any civilian unrest as a threat to be neutralized with lethal force.

**Colony Resource Matrix (The Resource Sheet):**
The Council (the players) must manage four primary resource pools, updated at the start of each "Colony Phase" (representing one week of game-time):

| Resource | Base Production (per week) | Weekly Consumption | Critical Threshold (0 Units) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rations (Food)** | +20 (Hydroponics Bay) | -30 (300 Colonists) | Starvation: Colonists gain exhaustion, morale drops by 10% daily. |
| **Water (Liters)** | +30 (Atmospheric Condensers)| -30 (Hydration requirements)| Dehydration: Construction halts, security units suffer penalty. |
| **Power (Megawatts)**| +50 (Geothermal Vent) | -40 (Fences, Habitats, Labs)| Grid Failure: Perimeter fences deactivate, local fauna attacks. |
| **Morale (Percent)**| Base: 70% | -5% (Hostile Environment) | Mutiny: Work strikes begin, security forces must establish martial law. |

## Act II: The First Winter

### Scene 1: The Frost-Storm
As the planet enters its ninety-day orbital winter, the temperature drops to -40 degrees Celsius. A massive storm of crystallized nitrogen sweeps down from the Mistcliffs, threatening to freeze the colony’s water lines and overload the primary power grid.

**The Mechanical Challenge (The Energy Crisis):**
The players must decide how to allocate their limited power output. They have 50 Megawatts of power, but the storm increases the colony's baseline needs:
- *Option A: Heat the Residential Domes (Cost: 20 MW).* Preserves Morale (+10%) and keeps colonists healthy. However, they must deactivate the Hydroponics Bay heaters (reducing Rations by 15).
- *Option B: Power the Perimeter Fences (Cost: 20 MW).* Keeps local wildlife—such as the territorial *Razor-Boars*—from breaching the camp. However, the Residential Domes drop to freezing temperatures, causing 10% of the colonists to fall sick.
- *Option C: Overload the Geothermal Vent.* A character can attempt a DC 15 Intelligence (Technology) check to bypass the reactor's safety protocols, generating an extra 20 MW of power.
  - *Success:* The colony is fully heated and secure.
  - *Failure:* The vent suffers a thermal rupture, permanently deactivating the power grid for three days and dropping Morale by 20%.

### Scene 2: The Silent Sabotage
While the Council struggles with the weather, a series of thefts occurs in the primary Ration Vault. Several crates of preserved proteins have been contaminated with a localized bio-toxin.

**The Investigation (Social/Security Challenge):**
The Council must identify the saboteur before a full panic erupts.
- *DC 13 Wisdom (Perception):* The locks on the Ration Vault were opened with an authorized engineering keycard.
- *DC 14 Intelligence (Medicine/Investigation):* The bio-toxin is an agricultural chemical used in the Hydroponics Bay to accelerate plant growth, pointing toward the New Dawn Association.
- *Suspect Interrogation:*
  - *Gunnar Vance* accuses the scientists of trying to starve the workers to force a return to Earth.
  - *Dr. Aria Thorne* swears the chemical was stolen from her lab, claiming that Commander Marcus Sterling's security team is trying to frame her association to justify a transition to martial law.

## Act III: The Alien Plague

### Scene 1: The Spore Cloud
During the spring thaw, a massive field of native purple fungi releases a dense cloud of microscopic spores that sweeps across the valley. The spores bypass the colony's primary air filtration systems, infecting sixty colonists with a rapid-onset respiratory rot known as "The Purple Lung."

**The Medical Crisis:**
The infected colonists must be quarantined inside the medical bay.
- *Dr. Thorne's Solution:* She can synthesize a vaccine using the enzyme of the local *Spore-Creeper* plant, which grows only inside the wet caves beneath the Mistcliffs. This requires a dangerous retrieval mission (see Act IV).
- *Commander Sterling's Solution:* He demands a strict, lethal quarantine of the infected sector, sealing the airlocks and letting the disease burn itself out to protect the remaining 240 colonists, saving resources but dropping Morale by 30%.
- *The Council's Dilemma:* Every day the vaccine is not synthesized, 1d10 colonists die, reducing the available labor force and dropping Morale.

## Act IV: The Retrieval Mission

### Scene 1: The Mistcliff Caves
The party must personally venture into the subterranean caverns beneath the Mistcliffs to retrieve the Spore-Creeper enzymes. The caves are a dark, humid maze of limestone pillars and bioluminescent moss.

**Cavern Hazards and Survival Checks:**
- *Slick Walls:* Climbing down into the cave shaft requires a DC 13 Strength (Athletics) check. On a failure, a character falls 20 feet into a pool of stagnant water, taking 2d6 bludgeoning damage and damaging their respiratory filters.
- *The Razor-Bat Nest:* The ceiling is lined with hundreds of territorial *Razor-Bats*—flying predators with wings of razor-sharp cartilage. The party must move silently (DC 13 Stealth) to avoid waking the swarm.

**The Monster of the Deep:**
The Spore-Creeper plants are guarded by the *Spore-Goliath*—a massive, centipede-like alien organism that feeds on the fungi.

*Combat Mechanics:*
- The Goliath is blind, navigating entirely by detecting heat and sound.
- *Acid Spray:* The Goliath can spray a 15-foot cone of corrosive acid that dissolves armor and flesh (dealing 3d6 acid damage).
- *Weakness:* The soft underbelly of the Goliath is vulnerable to fire. If a character can lure the creature over an open gas vent and ignite it, they can deal double damage.

## Act V: The Corporate Ultimatum

### Scene 1: The Arrival of Aegis-1
A massive corporate transport ship, the *Aegis-1*, arrives in orbit. The corporate executive, *Director Elizabeth Vance* (Gunnar Vance's sister, though she is loyal only to the company), lands with a squad of heavily armed security forces.

**The Ultimatum:**
Aegis Dynamics will provide unlimited food, power supplies, and medical equipment to fully cure Colony-9. However, in exchange, the Council must sign the "Colonial Charter Amendment," which:
1. Strips the Council of all legislative authority, transferring power to Aegis corporate representatives.
2. Converts the colony into a private mining facility, forcing all colonists (including scientists and children) into 12-hour work shifts in the toxic ore quarries.
3. Classifies any refusal to work or protest as "industrial sabotage," punishable by exile into the untamed wilderness.

**The Final Resolution (The Three Paths):**
The Council must present their decision to the colonists at a town hall meeting.
- **Path 1: The Corporate Surrender.** The Council signs the charter. The colony is saved from the plague and starvation, but the dream of a free frontier is dead. The colonists become indentured servants, and Gunnar’s Union begins plotting an armed worker rebellion.
- **Path 2: The Sovereign Declaration.** The Council refuses the charter, declaring Colony-9 a sovereign, independent settlement. This triggers a civil war: Director Vance orders her security forces to seize the ration vaults, forcing the players to lead the colonists in an armed defense of their home.
- **Path 3: The Terraforming Compromise.** A character can attempt a difficult negotiation, using the Spore-Creeper vaccine and their mining reports to prove that a self-sustaining scientific colony will yield 20% more long-term profit for the corporation than a forced labor camp. This requires a series of three successful DC 15 checks (Persuasion, Technology, and History) made in sequence.
  - *Success:* Aegis agrees to a joint partnership, preserving the Council's freedom while securing the colony's supply lines.
  - *Failure:* Director Vance views the negotiation as a stalling tactic and orders an immediate military takeover of the station.

## Game Master’s Reference and Colony Event Table

Use these tables to manage the weekly phases and systemic crises of Colony-9.

### Weekly Colony Event Table (Roll 1d6 at the start of each phase):
1. **Solar Flare:** Power grid production increases by +20 MW, but long-range communications are disabled. Hydroponics bays run at double efficiency.
2. **Water Leak:** A primary water line ruptures. Water resources drop by -20. Construction projects are delayed by three days.
3. **Labor Strike:** Tension between Gunnar Vance and the Council flares. Work halts in the mines. Ore production drops by 50% for one week.
4. **Fauna Migration:** A herd of giant *Aegis Elks* migrates through the valley. The colony's hunters can gather +30 Rations, but the elks damage several outer fences.
5. **Corporate Gift:** Aegis Dynamics drops a supply pod containing +20 Rations and +10 Water, but demands a detailed report on the colonists' psychological profiles.
6. **Equipment Failure:** The primary atmospheric condenser shuts down. Water production drops to 0 until an engineer makes a successful DC 13 Technology check.

### Colony Defense and Security Unit Matrix
- **Aegis Security Trooper:** HP 25, AC 14 (Tactical Armor). Equipped with a laser carbine (range 60/120 ft, deals 1d10 fire damage).
- **Automated Laser Turret:** HP 35, AC 16 (Reinforced Steel). Automatically fires at any target that breaches the perimeter fences, dealing 2d10 fire damage (DC 13 Dexterity save for half).
- **Perimeter Fence:** AC 12, 50 HP per section. Requires 10 MW of power to remain active. While active, any creature touching the fence takes 2d6 lightning damage.

Use this systemic guide to run a thrilling, strategic, and high-stakes colony-management campaign. Keep your resources tight, your factions tense, and the survival of Colony-9 ever in the balance!

## Act VI: Advanced Colony Economics and Industrial Development

To truly capture the depth of a colony-management simulation, the Game Master should employ a detailed economic system, industrial construction projects, and trade matrices. This section expands the physical facilities, raw resource refinements, and trade systems of Colony-9.

### Industrial Facilities and Infrastructure Upgrade Tree

The Colonial Council has the authority to construct several specialized facilities using *Titanium Ore* and *Steel Girders* harvested from the surrounding hills. Upgrading the colony's infrastructure unlocks new passive production bonuses and decreases weekly consumption rates.

#### 1. The Geothermal Core (Energy Facility)
- **Construction Cost:** 100 Units of Iron Ore, 50 Units of Electronics.
- **Construction Time:** 2 Weekly Phases.
- **Passive Benefit:** Generates a permanent +50 Megawatts of power per week. This power is unaffected by planetary winter storm cycles, providing absolute energy security.
- **Strategic Value:** Unlocks the *Deep-Mine Sinking Project*, allowing the colony to mine rare minerals (such as uranium and lithium) from the lower crust of Aegis Prime.

#### 2. The Bio-Dome Complex (Agricultural Facility)
- **Construction Cost:** 80 Units of Titanium Ore, 60 Units of Bio-Soil.
- **Construction Time:** 2 Weekly Phases.
- **Passive Benefit:** Produces +40 Rations per week. Decreases the risk of crop disease failures by 50%.
- **Strategic Value:** Allows the botanists to domesticate local plant species, producing rare medicinal extracts that grant immunity to regional airborne spore pathogens.

#### 3. The Water Reclamation Grid (Utility Facility)
- **Construction Cost:** 60 Units of Iron Ore, 40 Units of Filtration Membranes.
- **Construction Time:** 1 Weekly Phase.
- **Passive Benefit:** Increases water production by +30 Liters per week by recycling greywater from the residential habitats.
- **Strategic Value:** Prevents water dehydration events during the dry summer seasons, keeping the labor force healthy and operating at 100% efficiency.

#### 4. The Fabricator Workshop (Production Facility)
- **Construction Cost:** 120 Units of Iron Ore, 80 Units of Electronics, 1 Central AI Subprocessor.
- **Construction Time:** 3 Weekly Phases.
- **Passive Benefit:** Allows the automated construction of defensive turrets, vehicle chassis, and advanced heavy weapons.
- **Strategic Value:** Decreases the resource cost of all other construction projects by 15% due to high-precision manufacturing.

## Act VII: The Trade and Exchange Matrix

Colony-9 is not entirely isolated. Once the space-elevator port is established, the Council can trade resources with orbital cargo haulers and neighboring research outposts.

### Trade Exchange Rates and Corporate Tariffs

The value of resources fluctuates based on seasonal demand and localized system shortages. Use this exchange matrix to calculate trade transactions.

| Resource Traded | Orbital Value (Credits) | Regional Demand | Special Trade Partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron Ore (per Ton)** | 50 Credits | Low (market is saturated) | *Consolidated Metals Inc.* (buys for 60 Credits) |
| **Uranium (per Kilogram)**| 200 Credits | High (power grids need fuel)| *orbital Station Horizon* (buys for 250 Credits)|
| **Rations (per Crate)** | 100 Credits | Medium (seasonal need) | *Independent Miner's Guild* (trades 2:1 for Water) |
| **Medicinal Herbs** | 300 Credits | Very High (due to plague) | *Apex Biotech* (offers 2 Units of Tech for 1 Medicine) |

#### Trade Negotiation Mechanics:
When trading with corporate haulers, the Council's primary negotiator can attempt a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) or Intelligence (History/Economics) check to alter the terms in the colony's favor.
- **Success:** Decreases the purchase price of imported goods by 15% or increases the sale price of colony exports by 15%.
- **Failure:** The trade is conducted at standard, high-tariff corporate rates, resulting in a loss of 10% Morale among the workers due to perceived exploitation.

## Act IX: Systemic Combat and Operational Defense Protocols

When the colony's perimeter is breached by local fauna or rival corporate forces, the Council must coordinate the defense of the facility using tactical grid mechanics.

### The Colony Tactical Grid

The facility is divided into four concentric security rings. Each ring must be defended to prevent damage to the primary life-support systems.

```
+---------------------------------------------+
|               Outer Ring:                   |
|       Fences, Turrets, Gateways             |
|  +---------------------------------------+  |
|  |             Mid Ring:                 |  |
|  |    Hydroponics, Ore Processors        |  |
|  |  +---------------------------------+  |  |
|  |  |           Inner Core:           |  |  |
|  |  |   Residential Domes, Reactor    |  |  |
|  |  +---------------------------------+  |  |
|  +---------------------------------------+  |
+---------------------------------------------+
```

#### Defense Mechanics:
- **Outer Ring Breach:** If the outer fences are collapsed or deactivated, the defensive turrets in that sector are destroyed. Enemy forces gain access to the Mid Ring.
- **Mid Ring Breach:** If the ore processors or hydroponics bays are occupied or damaged during combat, their production drops to 0 for two weeks, and repairing them costs 50 Units of raw materials.
- **Inner Core Breach:** If enemies reach the residential domes, civilian casualties occur. Morale drops by 5% per round of combat, and any damage to the central reactor triggers a colony-wide power failure.

Use these advanced systems, upgrade trees, and trade matrices to construct a living, breathing colony where every choice has a systemic consequence, and every player's decision determines the survival of Colony-9!

## Act XI: Colony Design Guidelines and Layout Mapping

The layout of Colony-9 has a direct impact on its defensive capabilities and resource transport efficiency. When designing your colony's physical layout, use these rules to structure the map.

### Layout Mechanics

1. **Facility Proximity:**
   - *Power Lines:* Any facility built more than 100 yards from the Central Geothermal Vent requires the construction of a Power Substation (Cost: 10 Materials). Without a substation, the facility suffers a permanent 10% efficiency penalty due to resistance drops.
   - *Water Lines:* Hydroponics bays must be built adjacent to the Water Reclamation Grid. If built in a separate sector, water consumption for that bay increases by 20% due to line loss.

2. **Structural Integrity (SI):**
   - Each facility dome has an SI rating of 100. If a sector takes damage during an event or combat, subtract the damage from the dome's SI.
   - *SI 75-99:* Light damage. No resource penalties, but maintenance costs double (requires 10 Materials per week to repair).
   - *SI 50-74:* Moderate damage. Facility operates at half efficiency. Heat leaks occur, dropping Residential Dome insulation and reducing Morale.
   - *SI 0-49:* Severe damage/Breach. Facility is deactivated. All contents inside (such as crops or research data) are lost. Colonists in that sector must be evacuated to other domes, causing overcrowding and dropping Morale by 10% daily.

Use this complete tracking worksheet and physical design framework to run a masterfully executed, high-stress, and deeply satisfying colony-management TTRPG campaign!

## Act XII: Structural Faction Quests and Detailed Character Sheets

To give the Colonial Council deeper interaction options and make the political balancing act feel organic, this section provides detailed faction-specific quests, leadership profiles, and custom character sheets.

### Faction Quest A: Gunnar's Gambit (Union of Excavators)
- **Hook:** Gunnar Vance approaches the Council with a complaint. The automated drilling rigs are constantly breaking down, forcing human excavators into deeper, unstable shafts to meet their mineral quotas.
- **The Challenge:** Gunnar wants the Council to allocate 30 Units of Electronics and 20 Megawatts of power to overhaul the automated drill line, reducing physical labor risks.
- **The Trade-off:**
  - *If Accepted:* The Union of Excavators’ faction satisfaction increases to "Loyal." The risk of future labor strikes drops to 0%. However, deactivating the 20 MW from other sectors drops Hydroponics production by 10 Rations.
  - *If Rejected:* The Union's satisfaction drops to "Resentful." Gunnar begins organizing a secret strike. At the start of the next phase, all mine production drops by 80% until their demands are addressed.

### Faction Quest B: Dr. Thorne’s Legacy (New Dawn Association)
- **Hook:** Dr. Aria Thorne has discovered a rare, bioluminescent moss growing on the subterranean cave walls that can absorb radiation and purify soil contaminants.
- **The Challenge:** She needs a research team of three colonists and 50 Units of raw materials to build a specialized *Bioremediation Lab* to cultivate the moss.
- **The Trade-off:**
  - *If Accepted:* The New Dawn Association's faction satisfaction increases to "Loyal." In three phases, the colony permanently gains a +20% bonus to soil fertility, increasing crop yields.
  - *If Rejected:* The Association's satisfaction drops to "Disappointed." They refuse to share their agricultural research files, locking advanced hydroponics upgrades for the rest of the winter.

Use these detailed faction profiles, resource worksheets, and systemic event logs to run a masterfully executed, high-stakes colony-management campaign!
